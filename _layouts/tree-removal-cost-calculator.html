What Affects Tree Removal Cost?

These six factors have the biggest impact on your final quote.

📏

Tree Height & Size

The single biggest cost driver. Taller trees require more time, crew members, and specialized equipment like bucket trucks or cranes.

🌲

Species & Wood Density

Hardwoods like oak take longer to cut than softwoods. Palm trees require specialized techniques. Dead trees can be cheaper but less predictable.

📍

Location & Accessibility

Trees near power lines, buildings, or on steep slopes require extra care and rigging — typically adding 20–50% to the base cost.

🌀

Number of Trunks

Multi-stemmed trees or trees with multiple large branches require more cuts and rigging work, increasing both time and labour.

🏙️

Your Location / Region

Urban areas and high cost-of-living regions command 30–60% higher rates than rural markets. Weather and seasonal demand also plays a role.

🔧

Additional Services

Stump grinding (+$150–$450), debris haul-away (+$50–$200), and emergency same-day service (+$200–$500) can significantly increase your bill.

5 Ways to Save on Tree Removal

01

Get 3+ Competing Quotes

Prices for the same job can vary by 40–60%. Getting multiple quotes is the single most effective way to lower your cost.

02

Schedule in Off-Season

Late winter and early spring (before leaves return) is the slow season for arborists. Discounts of 15–25% are common.

03

Bundle Multiple Trees

If you have several trees to remove, doing them in one visit reduces per-tree mobilisation costs significantly.

04

Keep the Wood

Offer to keep the logs and wood chips. This saves the crew haul-away time and can reduce your quote by $100–$300.

05

Check Insurance Coverage

If a storm damaged the tree, your homeowner's insurance may cover some or all of the removal cost. Always file a claim first.

Frequently Asked Questions

The national average tree removal cost in 2026 is approximately $871, with most homeowners spending between $250 and $1,500. Small trees under 30 ft average $325, while very large trees over 80 ft can cost $2,000–$5,000 or more. Location, species, and accessibility are the biggest variables.
Homeowner insurance typically covers tree removal only when a tree falls due to a covered peril (storm, lightning, wind) and damages a covered structure like your home, fence, or car. Removal of a standing hazardous tree or a dead tree is generally not covered unless your policy has specific riders.
Permit requirements vary significantly by city and state. Many municipalities require permits for removing trees above a certain trunk diameter (commonly 6–12 inches DBH) or trees in protected zones. Your arborist should know local regulations, but always check with your local authority. Permit fees typically range from $50–$150.
A small tree can be removed in under an hour. A medium tree typically takes 2–4 hours. Large or complex trees — especially those near structures or power lines — can take a full day or more. Emergency removals after storms often take longer due to the unpredictable nature of fallen limbs.
DIY tree removal is possible for very small, isolated trees, but it is extremely dangerous and not recommended for trees taller than 15 feet. Without proper equipment and training, the risk of injury, property damage, and liability is significant. Professional arborists carry insurance — if something goes wrong with a DIY removal, you're fully liable.
